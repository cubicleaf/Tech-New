## 1. Washington forces Anthropic to kill Fable 5, then the shutdown unravels
**Anthropic · June 15, 2026 · 1 day ago · Still developing**

On June 13 the government ordered Anthropic to disable Fable 5 and Mythos 5 for foreign nationals, citing national security risk from an alleged jailbreak. Anthropic complied but disputes the severity — the technique was reportedly just instructing the model to "fix this code" on a flagged repo. By June 15, researcher Jamieson O'Reilly had reconstructed Fable 5's behavior inside Opus 4.8 using a leaked 120,000-character system prompt.

*Why it matters:* This is the first time Washington has forced a frontier US lab to pull a shipped model, and "resurrecting" it with one line of code undercuts the case that access controls mean anything post-deployment.

## 2. SpaceX goes public in the largest IPO ever, then launches two days later
**SpaceX · June 14, 2026 · 2 days ago · Still developing**

SpaceX began trading on Nasdaq as SPCX on June 12 at $135 a share, raising $75B and closing its first day up 19% at a roughly $1.77T valuation — the largest IPO on record. Two days later it flew its first Falcon 9 mission since going public.

*Why it matters:* A launch company is now among the ten largest public companies in the US, putting Musk's space business under quarterly-earnings scrutiny for the first time.

## 3. OpenAI confidentially files for an IPO
**OpenAI · June 8, 2026 · 8 days ago · Still developing**

OpenAI submitted a confidential Form S-1 to the SEC, the first concrete step toward a public listing, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley reportedly set to lead a possible fall offering. The filing follows a round valuing OpenAI at $852B and will eventually force public revenue disclosure.

*Why it matters:* With SpaceX freshly public and Anthropic fighting a government shutdown, AI's biggest names are converging on Wall Street at once, about to expose how much of the boom's economics hold up.

## 4. Apple admits Siri needs Google and Nvidia to compete
**Apple · June 12, 2026 · 4 days ago · Still developing**

Apple closed WWDC 2026 confirming its rebuilt Siri draws on Google's Gemini for frontier reasoning and Nvidia GPUs for Private Cloud Compute, while insisting it still trains its own on-device models. Reporting since the keynote says Apple plans to start charging for Apple Intelligence this year.

*Why it matters:* Apple just conceded it can't build a frontier model alone — outsourcing Siri's brains to two rivals while still charging for it bets that privacy framing matters more than owning the model.

## 5. IBM has an LLM discover hundreds of new quantum error-correction codes
**IBM Research · June 13, 2026 · 3 days ago**

IBM's open-sourced OpenEvolve framework used an LLM-guided evolutionary search to surface 465 new quantum error-correction codes in the bivariate bicycle family, automating work that previously required slow manual algebra.

*Why it matters:* Error correction is the main blocker between noisy qubits and fault-tolerant quantum computers — a concrete case of AI speeding up hard science rather than just generating text.

## Under the radar
