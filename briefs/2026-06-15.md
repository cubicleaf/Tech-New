*A single government directive Friday night proved that export controls now reach frontier AI models — and that a lab can be forced to kill its flagship product within hours of the order.*

## 1. US forces Anthropic to shut down Fable 5 and Mythos 5 globally
**Anthropic / US Government · June 12–13, 2026 · 2 days ago**

On June 12 at 5:21 PM ET, the US government issued an export control directive barring any foreign national — including Anthropic's own foreign-national employees inside the US — from accessing Fable 5 and Mythos 5, the frontier models launched just three days earlier on June 9. Complying with the order required Anthropic to disable both models for all customers worldwide; all other Claude models remain available. The government cited national security but provided no specific technical evidence; Anthropic says it believes the concern is a narrow jailbreak unlocking Mythos 5's cybersecurity capabilities in one specific scenario, not a universal bypass of Fable 5's safeguards, and is working to restore access.

*Why it matters:* This is the first time a US government directive has forced a frontier AI lab to pull its flagship model from global availability — it establishes a precedent that AI capability can be treated as a controlled munition, and every frontier lab now has to design for the possibility of an overnight forced shutdown.

## 2. Microsoft Majorana 2 claims 1,000x qubit stability gain; external physicists unconvinced
**Microsoft · June 2–3, 2026 · 13 days ago · Still developing**

At Build 2026, Microsoft unveiled Majorana 2, a topological quantum processor built with a lead-based superconducting materials stack replacing the aluminum used in its predecessor. The company claims parity lifetimes have improved from milliseconds to over 20 seconds — a roughly 1,000-fold gain — and that the chip, currently at 12 qubits, puts a scalable fault-tolerant machine on track by 2029, halving Microsoft's prior estimate. External physicists reviewing the preprint say the device data does not show repeatable results at scale, echoing the controversy that surrounded Majorana 1's topological qubit claims in 2023.

*Why it matters:* If the lifetime gains hold up under scrutiny, the timeline for cryptographically relevant quantum computing compresses by years; if they don't, the pattern of overstated milestones will further erode credibility for the entire field.

## 3. Google Gemini 3.5 Pro still unshipped; Flash already outpacing frontier rivals
**Google · Ongoing · Still developing**

Google announced Gemini 3.5 Pro at I/O on May 19 and told the audience it would ship "next month" — reportedly drawing audible groans. As of June 15, Pro has not appeared. Gemini 3.5 Flash, which did ship at I/O at $1.50 input / $9.00 output per million tokens, is scoring 55 on the LMSYS Intelligence Index versus Claude Sonnet 4.6 at 52 and Grok 4.3 at 53, and generates output at 284 tokens per second — roughly four times faster than comparable frontier models. Pro is expected to land a 2-million-token context window and a "Deep Think" reasoning mode at approximately $15/$60 per million tokens.

*Why it matters:* Flash's benchmark lead is already shifting developer routing decisions; Pro, when it ships, will determine whether Google reclaims the top reasoning tier that Anthropic and OpenAI have dominated.

## 4. DOE releases first unified national fusion strategy; Focused Energy closes $240M
**DOE / Focused Energy · June 9–10, 2026 · 5–6 days ago**

The Department of Energy published its Fusion Science and Technology Roadmap on June 9 — the first document to unify federal labs, universities, and private companies under a single national commercialization strategy targeting commercial grid power by the 2030s. The same week, Texas-based Focused Energy closed an oversubscribed $240 million Series A for its laser inertial confinement fusion approach, bringing total private capital raised to $300 million. Commonwealth Fusion Systems and Singapore's A*STAR also announced a five-year technology partnership focused on fusion plant components.

*Why it matters:* Coordinated federal strategy plus the largest private fusion raise of the year in the same week signals that fusion is transitioning from indefinite-horizon science into a funded commercial buildout phase, with timelines now measured in years rather than decades.

## 5. Colorado rewrites and delays its AI Act; effective date pushed to January 2027
**Colorado Governor Polis · May–June 2026 · Still developing**

On May 14, Governor Polis signed SB 189, which repealed and replaced Colorado's original 2024 AI Act rather than allowing it to take effect June 30. The replacement is significantly narrower: it drops the "high-risk AI systems" framing in favor of "automated decision-making in consequential decisions," reduces employer obligations, removes several transparency requirements, and delays the effective date to January 1, 2027. The original law had already survived two deadline extensions, a federal court injunction, a DOJ intervention, and a lawsuit from Elon Musk before being overhauled.

*Why it matters:* Colorado's law was the closest the US has come to a comprehensive state-level AI accountability framework; its effective gutting under industry pressure signals that meaningful AI liability obligations at the state level will be far harder to implement than advocates assumed.

## Under the radar

**HHS deploys ChatGPT to scan all 50 state Medicaid audits for $100–200B in fraud**
**HHS · June 2026**

The Department of Health and Human Services is using ChatGPT to analyze Medicaid audit reports across all 50 states, targeting an estimated $100–200 billion in potential fraud. The stated dollar scope makes this the largest disclosed government deployment of a commercial LLM by financial impact. No details on accuracy validation or human review process have been made public.

*Why it matters:* Federal adoption of off-the-shelf commercial LLMs for high-stakes audit functions at this scale will pressure procurement and liability frameworks — and set a precedent for what counts as adequate AI oversight in government.

**Atom Computing achieves first continuous multi-round quantum error correction on neutral atoms**
**Atom Computing · June 2026**

Atom Computing demonstrated a toric code configuration on its neutral-atom quantum computer showing reduced logical error rates as physical qubit count increases — the first continuous, multi-round error correction on this architecture. Neutral-atom systems have lagged superconducting platforms from IBM and Google on error correction milestones until now.

*Why it matters:* Neutral-atom platforms can operate at room temperature and scale more cheaply than cryogenic superconducting systems; this milestone puts them meaningfully in the race for fault-tolerant quantum computing.
