## 1. China orders Meta to unwind its $2B acquisition of agentic AI startup Manus

**China NDRC · April 27, 2026 · 1 day ago**

China's National Development and Reform Commission issued an order Monday demanding Meta unwind its acquisition of Manus, the autonomous AI agent startup originally founded in China and later headquartered in Singapore. Beijing opened a probe in January after a wave of domestic backlash accused Manus of "selling out" to the U.S.; the Monday order is one of China's most aggressive extraterritorial deal interventions on record. Manus drew wide attention last year for its autonomous agent that executes multi-step tasks on behalf of users.

*Why it matters:* Beijing is asserting that Chinese-founded AI companies—wherever they relocate—remain subject to national interest claims. This directly complicates acquisition strategies for any agentic AI startup with Chinese founders or early capital.

## 2. OpenAI GPT-5.5 is the first fully retrained base model since GPT-4.5

**OpenAI · April 23, 2026 · 5 days ago · Still developing**

GPT-5.5, released April 23, is the first model since GPT-4.5 rebuilt entirely from scratch rather than patched incrementally. On OSWorld-Verified (autonomous desktop task execution) it scores 78.7%, above the 72.4% human expert baseline. On Terminal-Bench 2.0 it reaches 82.7%, narrowly clearing Anthropic's Mythos Preview at 82.1%. The sharpest jump is long-context: MRCR v2 at 1M tokens climbs from 36.6% (GPT-5.4) to 74.0%.

*Why it matters:* A full architectural retrain means capability improvements compound differently than patches—the 2x long-context gain alone changes which autonomous agent workflows are tractable, with direct pricing implications for enterprise agentic deployments.

## 3. NVIDIA releases GR00T and Cosmos open models as physical AI enters deployment phase

**NVIDIA · April 14, 2026 · 14 days ago · Still developing**

During National Robotics Week NVIDIA open-sourced new Isaac GR00T models (robot manipulation via natural-language and vision-language-action reasoning) alongside Cosmos world foundation models (physics-aware simulation for pre-deployment training). Partners deploying the new models include Boston Dynamics, Caterpillar, Franka Robotics, and NEURA Robotics. Toyota Research Institute used Cosmos to achieve state-of-the-art results on teleoperation data augmentation and navigation world models.

*Why it matters:* Free open models for robot task generalization remove the per-lab R&D barrier; the Toyota Cosmos result suggests sim-to-real transfer is approaching the transfer fidelity needed for industrial deployment at scale.

## 4. Ineffable Intelligence raises $1.1B seed at $5.1B valuation—no product, all team

**Ineffable Intelligence · April 26, 2026 · 2 days ago**

David Silver—DeepMind researcher behind AlphaGo and AlphaZero—raised $1.1B in seed funding for his London-based lab Ineffable Intelligence at a $5.1B valuation, with no announced product or model. Silver's stated research direction is AI capable of genuine scientific discovery, which he frames as requiring deep RL rather than next-token prediction.

*Why it matters:* A $5.1B pre-product valuation is a direct read on how much capital is betting that deep RL is the next architectural layer beyond transformers—comparable in structure to Ilya Sutskever's SSI raise in 2024 and the bet it signals on post-transformer paradigms.

## 5. Horizon Robotics debuts first 5nm chip integrating cabin AI and autonomous driving

**Horizon Robotics · April 22, 2026 · 6 days ago · Still developing**

Chinese AV chipmaker Horizon Robotics launched its first 5nm automotive-grade processor that combines smart-cabin computing and autonomous driving on a single die, targeting the cost multiple that Nvidia and Qualcomm's multi-chip approaches require. The chip is positioned to help automakers cut BOM costs in China's intensifying EV price war.

*Why it matters:* Horizon is fabbing at SMIC's 5nm node, demonstrating that Chinese chipmakers can close process gaps for domain-specific workloads even with ASML export restrictions—integrated AV silicon is exactly where U.S. chip controls cannot follow.
