## 1. US forces Anthropic to shut down Fable 5 and Mythos globally over jailbreak
**Anthropic / US Commerce Dept · June 12–13 · 5–6 days ago · Still developing**

The US Commerce Department issued an export control directive on June 12 barring Anthropic from distributing Fable 5 and Mythos 5 to any foreign national, citing a jailbreak technique—reportedly used by a Chinese group—that unlocked Mythos's advanced cyber-offensive capabilities. Anthropic shut down both models globally, including for its own foreign-born staff, and met with the Trump administration on June 15 to contest the severity assessment. The company argues the same jailbreak can elicit comparable capabilities from GPT-5.5, which faces no equivalent restriction, undercutting the government's targeting logic.

*Why it matters:* This is the first time US export controls have been applied to force a global suspension of an already-deployed frontier AI system, establishing a template for government kill-switch authority over commercial AI.

## 2. Altman, Amodei, Hassabis pitch G7 leaders on US-led AI standards coalition
**Anthropic · Google DeepMind · OpenAI · June 17 · 1 day ago**

Dario Amodei, Demis Hassabis, and Sam Altman addressed G7 heads of state in a closed-door session in France on June 17, proposing a formal international body to set AI testing standards, share capability analyses, and coordinate chip trade that explicitly excludes China from frontier model access. No binding commitments emerged; Canadian PM Mark Carney expressed the most direct support, agreeing the US should lead.

*Why it matters:* Three competing lab CEOs presenting a unified governance framework to sitting heads of state signals AI policy is moving from domestic regulation into geopolitical architecture.

## 3. PsiQuantum breaks ground on first utility-scale quantum computer in Australia
**PsiQuantum · June 17 · 1 day ago**

PsiQuantum broke ground at Moreton Bay Central in Queensland on June 17, beginning construction of a silicon-photonic quantum computing facility backed by AUD $940 million in joint government-private funding. The site will house tens of thousands of photonic chips behind a Linde industrial cryoplant targeted for delivery in H2 2026, with computing cabinets following commissioning.

*Why it matters:* Moving from chip-level demos to a building-scale cryogenic facility is the step that separates credible quantum infrastructure from perpetual roadmap claims.

## 4. US closes Nvidia Blackwell China loophole exploited via Southeast Asia subsidiaries
**US BIS · June 1 · Still developing**

The Bureau of Industry and Security issued guidance on May 31 clarifying that Blackwell-class chips destined for entities whose ultimate parent is headquartered in China or Macau require export licenses regardless of where the purchasing subsidiary sits. The rule had existed since November 2023 but went unenforced, enabling hundreds of thousands of GB200 and AMD MI350x servers to flow through Singapore, Malaysia, and UAE front companies to Chinese-controlled data centers.

*Why it matters:* Closing the loophole tightens China's access to cutting-edge AI compute at the exact moment both countries are scaling reasoning-model and agent deployments.

## 5. Microsoft launches seven in-house MAI models, begins reducing OpenAI dependency
**Microsoft · June 2–3 · ~2 weeks ago**

At Microsoft Build 2026, Microsoft unveiled seven models under the MAI label: MAI-Thinking-1, a medium-scale reasoning model preferred to Claude Sonnet 4.6 in blind human evaluations on software engineering benchmarks; MAI-Code-1-Flash, built for agentic coding inside GitHub Copilot and VS Code; and MAI-Image-2.5 and MAI-Transcribe-1.5 rounding out the multimodal stack. Microsoft described the effort as "a hill-climbing machine" of continuous internal iteration.

*Why it matters:* Vertical integration of training, inference, and product embedding—running on Azure with no OpenAI royalty—reshapes the economics of the Microsoft-OpenAI partnership that has defined enterprise AI since 2019.
